Is a Caucasian right for me?

An honest talk before you take a puppy, for the next 10–12 years.

Better to ask before than after

The Caucasian Shepherd is a wonderful dog, but it is not for everyone. We would rather tell you this now than see a big dog looking for a new home a year from now. Caucasians bond with one family for life, and a change of owner is very hard for them.

That is why, when someone comes to us for a puppy, we first have a coffee and talk. Here is what about.

A Caucasian is for you if...

  • You have a house with a large, well-fenced yard.
  • You want a real guardian of your home and family, not a dog to play with everyone.
  • You are ready to spend time on training, especially in the first year.
  • You can provide food, vet care and attention for a big dog for 10–12 years.
  • You have experience with large dogs, or are willing to learn and ask.

Think again if...

  • You live in a flat or in a house without a fenced yard.
  • You want a dog you can let loose in the park among people and other dogs.
  • You travel often and the dog would stay with different people.
  • You expect a dog that obeys without question from day one.
  • Your neighbours find barking hard to bear.

The yard and the fence

A Caucasian is made to guard territory. It needs a yard it sees as its own and a fence that is high and strong. The fence protects both the dog and the people outside. A guardian makes no difference between a thief and a curious child who has walked in through an open gate.

Think also about where the dog will be when you have guests. An enclosure or a separate part of the yard solves many problems.

A guardian barks

A Caucasian warns with a bark when it senses a stranger, and that includes the night. For a guardian this is the job, not a bad habit. If your neighbours are close, think now about how they will take it.

Time and training

A Caucasian has a firm, independent character. For centuries it made its own decisions in the mountains. So you need to win its respect while it grows up. That takes time, patience and consistency.

The breed matures slowly. Males often still behave like adolescents after their first year. Early socialisation matters too. We wrote about it separately in puppy socialisation.

Costs

A big dog does not eat "a whole cow". With us an adult dog is fed once a day with about 700 g to 1 kg of food, a mix of quality dry food and raw meat. Add vaccinations, parasite treatment, check-ups and unexpected vet bills. What the puppy itself costs and what the price depends on, we explain in how much a Caucasian Shepherd costs.

The heat

A Caucasian is made for the cold. Heat is much harder for it. In summer you need shade in the yard, fresh water and walks only in the cool hours. The coat also needs regular brushing. We describe how in our article on coat and shedding.

Children and other animals

A Caucasian can live with children and with other animals, especially if it grows up with them. But it takes care and clear rules. Read more in Caucasian Shepherd and children.

If you are still unsure

Come and see us in Topolovo. See the dogs in person, see how big they are and how they behave, and ask all your questions. If after the talk you decide a Caucasian is not for you, that is a good outcome too. If you decide it is, the next step is to choose a puppy.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Caucasian Shepherd live in a flat?

It is not suitable. The Caucasian is a big guardian dog made to guard territory. It needs a house with a large, well-fenced yard.

Is a Caucasian suitable for someone without experience with dogs?

It can be, if you are ready to learn, to spend time on training and to ask. The Caucasian has a firm, independent character, so for a first one a female puppy is often the better choice.

Does a Caucasian bark a lot?

It warns with a bark when it senses a stranger, including at night. For a guardian this is the job, not a bad habit, so think about your neighbours in advance.

How much does it cost to keep a Caucasian Shepherd?

An adult dog with us eats about 700 g to 1 kg of food a day. On top of food come vaccinations, parasite treatment, check-ups and unexpected vet bills over 10–12 years.

Can a Caucasian get used to a new owner?

Only with great difficulty. Caucasians bond with one family for life, and an adult male almost never accepts a new owner. That is why it is important to decide honestly before you take a puppy.
