How to choose a Caucasian Shepherd puppy

What we look at when we assess a litter, and what you should ask.

They all look the same

When you see a litter of Caucasian Shepherds, every puppy looks equally fluffy and sweet. But even when they are small there are differences between them. Some you can see with the naked eye, and others the breeder will show you, because the breeder has known each puppy since its first day.

Here is what we look at, and what we advise you to look at too. Before that, it is worth choosing the right kennel. We wrote about that in how to choose a breeder.

First decide what the dog is for

Most people want a friend for the family and a guard for the home. For them a puppy from the standard pick is more than enough. It has the same parents and the same documents.

If you want a dog for shows or for breeding, you look much more strictly and usually want the first pick of the litter. That is why its price is higher. We explain how the price is set in how much a Caucasian Shepherd costs.

Male or female

Caucasian Shepherd males grow bigger and more massive. Under the FCI standard the desirable height at the withers is 72–75 cm for males and 67–70 cm for females, and the minimum weight is 50 and 45 kg.

A female, however, is just as good a guard and is often calmer and easier to train. If this is your first Caucasian Shepherd, think seriously about a female puppy.

Head and bone

A powerful head and strong bone are the trademark of the breed, and they show even in a small puppy. A good puppy is heavy for its age, with thick legs, a broad chest and a broad head.

Under the FCI standard, a build that is too light, weak bone and a head that is small for the body, narrow or long are severe faults. That is why the biggest puppy with the strongest bone and the most massive head is usually the first pick with us.

The bite

The standard requires a scissor or pincer bite and full dentition of 42 teeth. Any deviation from this bite disqualifies the dog from shows.

The most common problem is an undershot bite, where the lower teeth sit in front of the upper ones. This is a serious fault. Ask the breeder to show you the puppy's bite and ask about the parents' bites. If the puppy is only meant as a family guard, such a fault does not stop it from being a wonderful dog, but the price should be lower accordingly.

Tail, eyes and colour

  • Tail: set high, sickle shaped or curled. A kinked tail is a serious fault, and a docked tail disqualifies the dog from shows.
  • Eyes: under the standard, dogs with blue, greenish or odd-coloured eyes and dogs with an inward-turned eyelid (entropion) are disqualified.
  • Colour: any solid, piebald or spotted colour is allowed. Black in any form, genetic blue and brown are not. So if someone offers you a "rare black Caucasian", be careful.
  • Male puppies: under the standard a male must have two normal testicles fully descended into the scrotum. Ask the breeder about this.

Temperament

The FCI standard lists a lack of self-confidence as a severe fault, and aggressive or overly shy dogs are disqualified. We look for a balanced puppy: curious, one that comes to people, does not hide and settles quickly after a strange noise.

The loudest puppy in the litter is not necessarily the best one, and the quietest is not necessarily shy. So ask the breeder. We see the puppies every day from birth and tell you openly what each one is like.

When the puppy comes home

Check that you get an FCI pedigree, a microchip, an EU pet passport and records of vaccinations and deworming. Up to 6 months of age the puppy eats three times a day. Most importantly, do not overfeed it, because fast weight gain puts strain on the joints. Read more about feeding and care in our guide to the Caucasian Shepherd.

Frequently asked questions

Should I choose a male or a female Caucasian Shepherd puppy?

Males grow bigger and more massive. Females are just as good guards and are often calmer and easier to train. If this is your first Caucasian Shepherd, a female puppy is a good choice.

How can I tell whether a puppy will grow big?

The surest sign is the parents and the bloodline. A good puppy is heavy for its age, with a broad head, thick legs and strong bone. The exact size of the adult dog, however, cannot be promised for certain.

What bite should a Caucasian Shepherd have?

Under the FCI standard the bite is scissor or pincer, with full dentition of 42 teeth. An undershot bite is a serious fault, and any deviation from the required bite disqualifies the dog from shows.

How do I choose a puppy with a good temperament?

Look for a balanced puppy that is curious, comes to people and settles quickly. See the parents' temperament and ask the breeder, who sees the puppies every day from birth.

Can I choose a puppy without seeing it in person?

It is best to come to the kennel and see the puppies and their mother. If you live far away or abroad, ask for photos and video of the puppy and the parents, the pedigree and written terms before you pay.

What to look at

  • The parents and their temperament
  • A massive head and strong bone
  • A scissor or pincer bite
  • Correct tail and colour under the standard
  • A curious, balanced puppy
  • The documents before payment
