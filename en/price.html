How much does a Caucasian Shepherd cost?

Why two puppies of the same breed can have very different prices, and what you are really paying for.

The first question we hear

“How much is the puppy?” That is the first thing people ask on the phone. The honest answer is that there is no single price. Two puppies from the same litter can cost differently, and there are good reasons for that.

Here we explain how the price of a Caucasian Shepherd is set with us and with any serious breeder. Then you can judge for yourself whether a listing is a good deal or a trap.

What listings in Bulgaria show

On Bulgarian classified sites, Caucasian Shepherd puppies are usually offered for about €500 to €1,300. You will also see listings for “€1”, but that is only a trick to get to the top of the search results.

The gap between €500 and €1,300 is not random. Behind it are the parents, the documents and the care the puppy gets in its first weeks.

1. The parents and the bloodline

What matters most for the price is where the puppy comes from. We look not only at the father and mother but at the whole family tree: the grandparents and their ancestors. How big they are, what character they have and which show titles they have won.

A puppy from champion parents with a well-known line behind them has a much better chance of becoming a big, beautiful and balanced dog. That is why it costs more. Our parent dogs hold many show titles, including BOB (Best of Breed) and CAC, and the kennel has won more than 200 first-place cups.

2. The puppy itself

Even within one litter, puppies are not the same. From an early age you can see which one is the biggest, which has the strongest bones, the most massive head and the most correct stance. The best puppy in the litter always costs the most.

3. Who picks first

When we have a litter, many people are interested. So there is a pick order:

  • First pick: the highest price, because this person takes the best, usually the biggest, puppy.
  • Second and third pick: the price gradually goes down.
  • Standard price: for the rest of the litter. It depends on the line and the pedigree.

A standard price does not mean a worse dog. It only means the most outstanding show puppies have already been chosen. Most people are not looking for a ring champion anyway, but for a friend and a guardian for their home. For that, a standard-pick puppy is more than enough.

4. Faults that lower the price

If a puppy deviates from the breed standard, the price is lower. How much lower depends on how serious the fault is.

Serious faults: the price drops sharply. An undershot (reverse) bite and a kinked tail are serious faults. A dog with such a fault is disqualified from shows, so its price is much lower.

Minor faults: the price is lower. For example, problems with the position of the feet. They lower the price, but not as much as the serious faults.

Either way, the dog can be a wonderful guardian and family member. We keep a high standard, so puppies like this are rare with us. But when there is one, we say so openly.

5. Male or female

Male Caucasian Shepherds grow bigger and more massive, and most people look for a male. That is why females are usually a little cheaper. A female, however, is just as good a guardian and is often calmer and easier to train.

What the price should include

When you compare prices, compare what you get as well. Every puppy from us leaves with:

  • an FCI pedigree;
  • vaccinations for its age and deworming;
  • a microchip and an EU pet passport;
  • advice on feeding, raising and training;
  • contact with us after the sale. We ask new owners to send photos so we can follow how the puppy grows and help with advice.

If a listing has no pedigree or passport, the lower price is only an illusion. Without a pedigree you cannot prove the dog is purebred or enter it in shows.

Deposit and reservation

If you like a puppy, you can reserve it with a deposit. It then stays yours until it is ready to go to its new home. If there are no puppies right now, you can sign up for the next litter. The earlier you do, the higher you are in the pick order.

Puppies abroad

Our puppies live in Greece, Italy, Serbia, North Macedonia, Romania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, England, Ukraine, Germany and even India. We do not have our own transport yet, so travel is at the buyer's expense. As an exception, if we have the time, we may deliver the puppy in person, with all transport costs paid by the buyer. This is agreed in advance.

How to avoid a scam

  • Check that the kennel is registered with the Bulgarian Food Safety Agency (BFSA). By law kennels must be registered, but few are. Ours is registered under No. 4260-0913.
  • Always ask to see the puppy's mother and, if possible, the father.
  • Ask for the pedigree and the passport before you pay. “The papers will come later” is a bad sign.
  • Do not send money for a puppy you have not seen to a person you do not know.
  • A price that is far too low for a Caucasian Shepherd almost always hides something: a sick puppy, a cross-breed or missing documents.

The price spread over the years

A Caucasian Shepherd lives 10–12 years on average. The difference between a cheap puppy and the right one looks big on the day you buy it, but spread over all the years the dog will guard your home, it is very small. A good start (parents, health and proper raising) pays back many times over.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Caucasian Shepherd puppy cost in Bulgaria?

Listings in Bulgaria usually ask between about €500 and €1,300. The price depends mainly on the parents and the bloodline, on the quality of the puppy itself, on the pick order in the litter and on whether it comes with an FCI pedigree, vaccinations, a microchip and a passport.

Why are female puppies cheaper?

Males usually grow bigger and more massive, so most people look for a male. With us, females are a little cheaper than males from the same litter, with the same quality and the same documents.

Can I reserve a puppy in advance?

Yes. A puppy is reserved with a deposit. The earlier you sign up, the higher you are in the pick order.

Do you sell puppies abroad?

Yes. Our puppies live in Greece, Italy, Serbia, North Macedonia, Romania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, England, Ukraine, Germany and even India. Transport is at the buyer's expense. As an exception, and when we can, we may deliver the puppy in person, with all transport costs paid by the buyer.

Why are some puppies sold so cheaply?

A very low price almost always means that the pedigree, vaccinations or passport are missing, or that the parents are not purebred. Listings for €1 are only bait. Ask to see the parents and the documents before you pay.
