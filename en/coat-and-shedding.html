The Caucasian Shepherd's coat

How it sheds, how to brush it and why it is not clipped even in the worst heat.

A coat made for the mountains

The thick coat is one of the first things people notice about a Caucasian Shepherd. It is not just for looks. For centuries this coat protected the dogs from cold, wind and snow in the Caucasus mountains.

The FCI standard describes the coat as straight, coarse and dense, with a well-developed undercoat. The outer coat and the undercoat are at least 5 cm long. In longer-coated dogs the males have a clear mane around the neck, and the hind legs carry "trousers".

Two layers

A Caucasian's coat has two layers. On top is the coarse outer coat, which sheds water and dirt. Underneath is the dense, soft undercoat, which keeps the dog warm like a down jacket.

It is the undercoat that falls out when the dog sheds. That is why the coat needs care: if the old undercoat is not brushed out, it mats, the skin cannot breathe and the dog feels hotter.

How much does a Caucasian shed?

Most of the time a Caucasian loses very little hair. But once or twice a year, most often in spring, it changes its undercoat and then it sheds heavily. For a few weeks a single dog then loses a surprising amount of hair.

Females often shed heavily after a heat or after whelping too. This is normal.

How to brush

  • Outside the shedding season: brush the dog once or twice a week.
  • While shedding: every day, until the old undercoat is out.
  • Tools: an undercoat rake, a metal comb with wide and narrow teeth and a slicker brush.
  • Direction: brush with the lie of the hair, section by section, down to the skin, not just over the surface.

Pay most attention behind the ears, along the mane, under the front legs and on the "trousers" of the hind legs. That is where the coat mats most easily. Work small mats loose with your fingers and a comb rather than pulling them.

Get the puppy used to brushing while it is small. An adult Caucasian that does not like being brushed is a hard job.

Do not clip a Caucasian

Many owners think they will help the dog in summer by clipping or shaving it. Do not do it. The outer coat protects the skin from the sun, and the coat insulates against both cold and heat. A clipped dog is more exposed to the sun, and the coat can grow back uneven and with a different texture.

The best help in the heat is regular brushing of the shedding undercoat, shade and fresh water. We wrote more about summer care in our guide to the Caucasian Shepherd.

Bathing

A Caucasian rarely needs a bath. The coarse coat cleans itself and does not hold dirt, and frequent baths strip the natural oils that protect it. Bathe the dog only when it is really dirty, with a dog shampoo, and dry it well, especially the undercoat.

Other care

  • Nails: if the dog does not wear them down itself, trim them.
  • Ears: check them regularly for dirt and a bad smell.
  • Paws: check the hair between the pads, and look for thorns and burrs after walks.
  • Parasites: fleas and ticks are hard to see in a thick coat, so treat the dog regularly as your vet advises.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Caucasian Shepherd shed?

Most of the time it loses little hair, but once or twice a year, most often in spring, it changes its undercoat and sheds heavily for several weeks. Females often shed heavily after a heat or whelping too.

How often should a Caucasian be brushed?

Once or twice a week outside the shedding season, and every day while it sheds, until the old undercoat is out.

Can a Caucasian Shepherd be clipped in summer?

It is not recommended. The coat protects the skin from the sun and insulates against both cold and heat. Regular brushing of the shedding undercoat, shade and fresh water help much more.

How often should a Caucasian Shepherd be bathed?

Rarely, only when the dog is really dirty. The coarse coat cleans itself, and frequent baths strip the natural oils that protect it.

What should I brush a Caucasian Shepherd with?

An undercoat rake, a metal comb and a slicker brush. Brush section by section, down to the skin, and pay attention behind the ears, along the mane and on the hind legs.

Coat care

  • Brushing 1–2 times a week
  • Every day while shedding
  • Undercoat rake and metal comb
  • No clipping or shaving
  • Bath only when needed
