How to choose a Caucasian Shepherd breeder

Seven checks that set a serious kennel apart from just another puppy ad.

You are choosing a kennel, not just a puppy

When you look for a Caucasian Shepherd, you are really choosing a kennel. It decides the parents, the puppy's first weeks, the documents and the help you get afterwards. And the dog will live with you for 10–12 years.

These are the questions we would ask if we were buying a puppy from another kennel. You can check every one of them yourself, without taking anyone's word for it. Ours included.

1. A kennel name registered with the FCI

Every serious breeder has a kennel name (affix) registered with the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI). It appears in front of the name of every puppy in the pedigree, and nobody else may use it.

The FCI keeps a public directory of registered kennel names. Pick the first letters of the name and check that the kennel is listed. Our name VIKAS-DOG was registered in 2013 under No. 03/2013 and is listed as VIKASDOG.

Also ask which organisation issues the pedigree. A pedigree recognised by the FCI is needed for FCI shows and for breeding by the rules.

2. Registration with the Bulgarian Food Safety Agency

By law, dog kennels in Bulgaria must be registered with the Bulgarian Food Safety Agency (BFSA). In practice few are: as of November 2026 there are only 134 registered dog kennels in the whole country.

The register is public. Open the BFSA register of kennels (in Bulgarian) and search by number or by village. The register has no breed field, so compare the kennel's name and address. Our number is BG100000105099 (vet. reg. No. 4260-0913), and we are the only registered dog kennel in Asenovgrad municipality.

3. See the mother, and the father if you can

The puppies' mother should be at the kennel and you should be able to see her. If you are told she is "not here" or cannot be shown, that is a bad sign.

The father sometimes comes from another kennel. Then ask for photos, his pedigree and his titles. Look not only at how big the parents are but at how they behave. Under the FCI standard the correct temperament is balanced, confident and fearless, and aggressive or overly shy dogs are disqualified. Temperament is inherited, so calm and confident parents are the best guarantee.

4. Visit in person

A good breeder has nothing to hide and will invite you over. See where the dogs live: whether the place is clean, whether the dogs look healthy and well kept, and whether the puppies are curious and bold with people.

Our dogs live outdoors in a family setting, in our yard in Topolovo. When someone comes for a puppy, we start with a coffee and talk about the Caucasian Shepherd, and only then show the dogs.

5. Documents before payment

Before you pay, you should know which documents you will get. The puppy should leave with:

  • a pedigree recognised by the FCI;
  • a microchip and an EU pet passport;
  • vaccinations for its age and deworming;
  • written terms of sale and guarantee.

"The papers will come later" is a bad sign. Read more about what the price should include in how much a Caucasian Shepherd costs.

6. Shows and titles

Titles are not everything, but they show that the kennel's dogs have been judged against the breed standard by judges, not only by their owner. Ask which titles the parents hold and ask to see the certificates.

Our dogs hold show titles including BOB (Best of Breed) and CAC. See photos from the Club Show 2026.

7. How the breeder treats you

A good breeder is in no hurry to sell. They will ask you questions too: do you have a yard, a fence, experience with large dogs, why exactly a Caucasian. This is not mistrust. They want the puppy to go to the right home, because a Caucasian bonds for life and finds it very hard to accept a new owner.

A good breeder also tells you the unpleasant things openly, for example when a puppy has a fault, and stays available after the sale while the puppy grows.

Warning signs

  • You cannot see the mother.
  • The seller has no registration and the papers "will come later".
  • You are asked to pay in advance for a puppy you have never seen, to a person you do not know.
  • The seller offers puppies of many different breeds at the same time.
  • Nobody asks a single question about you or where the dog will live.
  • The price is unusually low. That alone does not prove a scam, but it is a reason to check more carefully.

Which is the best Caucasian Shepherd kennel?

There is no official ranking of kennels in Bulgaria. Anyone can write on their website that they are "the best", us included. So trust the checks, not the words.

The best kennel for you is the one that passes every check above, whose dogs you like in type and temperament, and whom you can talk to openly years from now.

Frequently asked questions

How can I check that a kennel is registered?

There are two checks. The kennel name should be in the FCI's public directory of kennel names on fci.be. Registration in Bulgaria is checked in the public register of kennels of the Bulgarian Food Safety Agency, by number or by village. Our number there is BG100000105099.

Is registration with the Bulgarian Food Safety Agency mandatory?

Yes. By law, dog kennels in Bulgaria must be registered with the Bulgarian Food Safety Agency. As of November 2026 there are only 134 registered dog kennels in the whole country.

Should I see the puppy's parents?

You should always be able to see the mother at the kennel. The father sometimes comes from another kennel, and then you should ask for photos, his pedigree and his titles. Look at the parents' temperament, not only their size.

Which documents should come with the puppy?

A pedigree recognised by the FCI, a microchip, an EU pet passport, records of vaccinations and deworming, and written terms of sale and guarantee. Ask for them before you pay.

Which is the best Caucasian Shepherd kennel in Bulgaria?

There is no official ranking and anyone can claim to be the best. The best kennel for you is one that is registered with the FCI and the Bulgarian Food Safety Agency, shows you the parents and the documents before payment, has dogs judged at shows and stays with you after the sale.
