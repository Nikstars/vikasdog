Caucasian Shepherd

An ancient guardian from the Caucasus mountains – brave, independent and endlessly devoted to its family.

Over coffee at the kennel

When someone comes to us for a puppy, we don't start with the price. First we make coffee, sit down in the yard and talk about the breed – its character, how it guards, how it bonds and what it needs.

We want to see whether the people and the Caucasian Shepherd are a good match. Having the money for a puppy is not enough – you need the space, the time and the means to raise a big, powerful guardian. This page is part of that conversation.

Origin

The Caucasian Shepherd descends from the ancient dogs of the Caucasus. For centuries it guarded the flocks and homes of people in the mountains of today's Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Dagestan and on the steppes of Southern Russia – against wolves, bears and strangers.

The earliest mention of large molossoid dogs in the region dates from the 1st century BC, in the army of the Armenian king Tigran II. Planned breeding began in the USSR in the 1920s. Breeders selected for exactly the qualities we value today: strength, self-confidence, fearlessness, sharp hearing and sight, and a dense, waterproof coat that lets the dog cope with the harshest climate.

Today the International Canine Federation (FCI) recognises the breed under standard No. 328, with Russia as the country of patronage.

A guardian that thinks

The Caucasian Shepherd has a reputation as a fierce dog. Its aggression, though, has one purpose – to guard. It is aimed at strangers and animals that come where they shouldn't.

What sets it apart is the way it acts. A Caucasian does not blindly attack whatever is in its way. It first reads the situation and then decides what to do.

If you are out with your Caucasian and a stray dog appears, it won't charge at it. It will stand by your side and guard you until the other dog attacks first. Smaller dogs that snarl at it are often simply ignored – it doesn't see them as a threat. Its main goal is not to attack but to keep you safe.

But if its owner is truly in danger, there is no better guardian. It will fight to the end for you. A Caucasian Shepherd is a whole security system, with real firepower, living in your yard.

The FCI standard describes exactly this: the behaviour should be steady, active, self-confident, fearless and independent, with a devoted attachment to its master. Aggressive or overly shy dogs do not meet the standard and are disqualified at shows. That is why, when raised well, the Caucasian is one of the most balanced breeds we know – it combines calm, devotion, intelligence and, when needed, strength.

One family for life

Caucasians bond extremely strongly. Once they accept a person or a family as their own, it is for life, and nobody can come between them.

That is why a change of owner is very hard for them. An adult male in particular finds it very difficult, sometimes almost impossible, to accept a new master. We have seen dogs take such a change hard – both mentally and physically.

It is also why we choose carefully who we give a puppy to. It is best for the dog to come to you young and grow up with your family.

With children and family

People often ask whether a Caucasian can live with a family with children. It can. Caucasians are intelligent and very rarely show aggression towards children, even ones they don't know. With the children of their own family they are usually calm and patient.

Still, this is a big and powerful breed, so always be careful:

  • never leave small children alone with the dog;
  • take care when other children visit – loud play and wrestling may look to the dog like an attack on "its own";
  • teach children to respect the dog – not to pull it or bother it while it eats or rests.

"It must eat a whole cow"

This is probably the question we hear most. The answer surprises most people.

At our kennel, a big adult dog eats once a day – about 700 g to 1 kg of food. It is a mix of quality dry food and raw meat made to our own recipe. Young dogs from 6 months to 1 year eat twice a day, and puppies under 6 months three times a day.

Large dogs with a deep chest are prone to stomach torsion (bloat). So don't walk the dog or let it run and play for an hour or two before and after a meal. If you prefer, the daily amount can also be split into two meals. And with a growing puppy, the most important thing is not to overfeed it – gaining weight too fast strains the joints.

Care through the year

The Caucasian is made for the cold – snow is its season. Heat is much harder on it.

  • Walks – in summer only in the cool hours: early morning or evening. Never walk it in the worst of the heat.
  • Water and shade – in hot weather change the water often so it stays fresh and cool, and make sure there is shade in the yard.
  • Coat – thick and long, so brush it regularly to remove the old coat and keep the dog cooler. Every day while it sheds.
  • Health – vaccinations, deworming and regular vet check-ups. A Caucasian lives on average 10–12 years.

Training: who is the boss

The Caucasian has a strong character and is independent – for centuries it made its own decisions in the mountains. So while it is growing up, you need to earn its respect and show it that you are the boss.

This takes time and patience, and with some dogs more effort. But once you have earned a Caucasian's respect, there is no going back.

Early socialisation matters too – the puppy should get to know people, sounds and other animals from an early age. The breed matures slowly, so be consistent.

Not for everyone

A Caucasian Shepherd needs attention, care and love to show its full potential. If you have a yard, the time and the will to raise it well, you will get a friend and a guardian you can rely on for the rest of its life.

Breed at a glance
Origin The Caucasus and the steppes of Southern Russia; FCI standard No. 328, patronage Russia
Purpose Guard and watchdog
FCI group Group 2, section 2.2 – Molossoid, mountain type
Height at withers Males – desirable 72–75 cm, at least 68 cm
Females – desirable 67–70 cm, at least 64 cm
Taller dogs are acceptable if harmoniously built
Weight Males – at least 50 kg
Females – at least 45 kg
Behaviour Steady, active, self-confident, fearless and independent; aggressive or overly shy dogs are disqualified
Coat Straight, coarse and dense, with a well-developed undercoat – guard hair and undercoat at least 5 cm; in longer-coated males a distinct mane
Colours Any solid, piebald or spotted colour – shades of grey, fawn, red, white. Black in any form, genetic blue and brown are not allowed
Life expectancy On average 10–12 years

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Caucasian Shepherd eat?

Much less than most people think. At our kennel a big adult dog eats once a day – about 700 g to 1 kg of food, a mix of quality dry food and raw meat. Young dogs from 6 months to 1 year eat twice a day, and puppies under 6 months three times.

Is a Caucasian Shepherd good for a family with children?

Yes, if it is raised well. Caucasians rarely show aggression towards children and are calm and patient with the children of their own family. But this is a big and powerful breed, so never leave small children alone with the dog and take care when other children visit.

Is the Caucasian Shepherd an aggressive breed?

The Caucasian is a guardian, not a blind attacker. It is wary of strangers and other animals, but it reads the situation first and attacks only when there is a real threat. Under the FCI standard aggressive dogs are disqualified – the correct behaviour is steady and self-confident.

Can a Caucasian get used to a new owner?

With great difficulty. Caucasians bond with one family for life, and an adult male almost never accepts a new master. That is why it is best for the puppy to come to you young and grow up with your family.

How does a Caucasian cope with heat?

The breed is made for the cold and heat is hard on it. In summer walk it only in the cool hours, change the water often, provide shade and brush the coat regularly to remove old hair.

How long does a Caucasian Shepherd live?

On average 10–12 years. Good food without overfeeding, exercise and regular vet check-ups help the dog stay healthy for longer.

How big does a Caucasian Shepherd get?

Under the FCI standard the desirable height at the withers is 72–75 cm for males and 67–70 cm for females, with a minimum weight of 50 and 45 kg. Taller dogs are acceptable if they are harmoniously built. Our record male stands 85 cm and weighs 116 kg.

Is it right for you?

  • You have a house with a large, well-fenced yard
  • You want a reliable guardian for home and family
  • You are ready to invest time in training
  • You can provide food, vet care and attention for a big dog
  • You have experience with large dogs or are willing to learn
