Puppy socialisation

How the first months at home turn a puppy into a balanced guardian.

Why socialisation matters so much in a Caucasian

By nature the Caucasian Shepherd is wary of anything unfamiliar. That makes it a good guardian. But a guardian has to be able to tell a real threat from the postman, from guests and from the noise of a passing lorry.

That skill is learned. A puppy that has got to know the world grows up confident and calm. A puppy raised shut away often grows up either fearful or suspicious of everything. The FCI standard asks for exactly the opposite: a balanced, confident and fearless dog. Aggressive or overly shy dogs are disqualified.

Socialisation does not kill the guardian

People often ask us whether a puppy that meets many people will become "everyone's friend" and stop guarding. No. The guarding instinct is in the breed's blood and shows as the dog matures. Socialisation does not remove it. It makes it sensible: the dog knows when there is a threat and when there is not.

The first weeks at the kennel

Socialisation starts with us. The puppies grow up in a family setting, among people, children and other animals. They get used to being picked up and stroked and to the everyday sounds of the yard. When the puppy comes to you, your job is to carry on.

The first days at home

In the first days let the puppy settle quietly into its new home, with you and the yard. Do not invite all your relatives and friends at once. Let it learn that it is safe here. Then start widening its world little by little.

What it should get to know

  • People: men and women, adults and children, people in hats, glasses, with umbrellas. Let them greet it calmly, without crowding it.
  • Sounds: the vacuum cleaner, the lawnmower, cars, thunder, the doorbell. At first from a distance and quietly, then closer.
  • Surfaces: grass, tiles, gravel, a metal grate, stairs.
  • The car: short trips that end with something nice, not only at the vet.
  • Other animals: the cats, birds and other dogs at home. A Caucasian that grows up with them accepts them as its own.
  • Handling: checking ears, teeth and paws, brushing, trimming nails. A big dog has to accept this calmly.

How to do it

  • Short and often: a few minutes every day are better than one long, tiring day.
  • Pleasant: let every new thing end with praise, play or a treat.
  • No force: if the puppy is scared, do not drag it towards the scary thing and do not punish it. Move away and try again later, from further off.
  • Consistent: the house rules are the same for everyone in the family.

Before the vaccinations are complete

Until the puppy has had all its vaccinations, do not take it to places where many dogs pass, such as parks and dog areas. Ask your vet when it is safe. In the meantime socialisation carries on in the yard, at home, in your arms or in the car.

The basic commands

From an early age teach the puppy to come when called by name, to sit and to walk on a lead. A Caucasian quickly becomes big and strong. What you teach the puppy at 3 months will help you when it weighs 50 kg. You will find more about training in our guide to the Caucasian Shepherd.

The breed matures slowly

A Caucasian matures slowly and its character forms gradually. So socialisation does not end after the first months. Keep taking it to new places and showing it new things while it grows. We are here with advice for the dog's whole life. If you are still choosing, read how to choose a puppy.

Frequently asked questions

When should socialisation of a Caucasian Shepherd puppy start?

Already at the kennel, where the puppies grow up among people, children and animals. When the puppy comes to you, let it settle for a few days, then gradually show it new people, sounds, places and animals.

Will a socialised Caucasian stop guarding?

No. The guarding instinct is in the breed's blood. Socialisation does not remove it but makes it sensible, so the dog can tell a real threat from ordinary everyday things.

Can the puppy go out before its vaccinations are complete?

Do not take it to places where many dogs pass, such as parks and dog areas, until your vet says it is safe. Socialisation can continue in the yard, at home and in the car.

What should I do if the puppy is afraid of something?

Do not force it and do not punish it. Move away from what scares it and try again later, calmly and from further off, ending with praise or a treat.

A little every day

  • New people, calmly and without pressure
  • Sounds, at first from a distance
  • Short car trips
  • Checking ears, teeth and paws
  • Coming when called and the lead
  • Parks only after the vaccinations
